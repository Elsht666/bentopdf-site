常見問題

How do I merge all pages of a PDF into one long page?

Upload the PDF, keep Layout Direction set to Vertical, and click the process button. Every page is placed top to bottom on one continuous page, with the output width matching the widest source page and narrower pages centered.

Can I put the pages side by side instead of stacked?

Choose Horizontal (Left to Right) in Layout Direction. Pages are laid out in order from left to right on a single wide page, and pages shorter than the tallest one are centered vertically.

Can I add a gap or a line between the pages?

Spacing (0 by default) adds a gap between pages, measured in the PDF's own units where 72 equals one inch, and Background Color fills that gap. Turn on Add Separator Lines to draw a line through the middle of each gap, with its own thickness (0.5 units and up, in steps of 0.5) and color.

Does the background color apply to the whole page or only the gaps?

It fills the whole output page underneath the source pages, so it shows in the spacing between pages and in any margin where a narrower page is centered. With white selected, the default, no fill is drawn at all.

Will the text in the combined page still be selectable?

No. Each source page is rendered to an image at 2x resolution and drawn onto the new page, so the result is a picture of your pages rather than live text. Keep the original PDF if you need to copy or search text later.

Is there a limit to how many pages I can stitch together?

The tool doesn't set one, but the output grows with every page you add: a 100-page document becomes a page roughly 100 pages tall. Some readers struggle with very large pages; Adobe Reader, for example, doesn't display pages larger than 200 inches on a side.

What is a single long-page PDF useful for?

It suits scrolling previews for the web, design mockups, comic strips, and storyboards where the reader scrolls instead of flipping pages. If you want several pages printed on one sheet in a grid, N-Up PDF is the better fit.

Does the combined PDF get uploaded to a server?

No. Rendering the pages and building the new single-page PDF both happen in your browser, and the file stays on your device the whole time.
